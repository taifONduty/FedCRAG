\section{A System Model of Federated Retriever Adaptation}\label{sec:model}

\noindent\textbf{Status.} The observations in \S\ref{sec:phenomenon} motivated this
model; they are therefore \emph{not} evidence for it. This section states the
model and derives predictions on quantities and configurations that played no
role in its construction. \S\ref{sec:tests} reports those tests. We label every
claim as \textsc{observation}, \textsc{assumption}, or \textsc{prediction}, and
no prediction is scored against data that motivated it.

\subsection{Setting and the server's observable}

$K$ silos adapt one dense retriever. In round $t$ client $k$ returns an adapter
delta $\Delta W_k$; the server applies $\Delta W_{\mathrm{agg}}=\sum_k
v_k\,\Delta W_k$. The server never sees documents, queries, or losses --- only
the deltas, and hence their Gram
$G_{ij}=\langle\Delta W_i,\Delta W_j\rangle_F$.

The Gram is exactly computable for \emph{any} fixed shared $A$ via
$G_{ij}=\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(\Delta B_i^\top \Delta B_j M_A)$ with
$M_A=AA^\top$, and remains exactly computable when both factors train, through
the four-term identity on $B^+A^+-BA$. \textbf{Freezing $A$ is therefore not
required to measure geometry, and we do not claim it is.} What the shared frozen
factor buys is exact \emph{application}: an arbitrary aggregate weight-space
step has no exact rank-$r$ factored representation, so realising a solved
direction $s\,d^\star$ in the communicated parameterisation requires it. Row
orthogonality is a conditioning choice on top, making factor-space geometry
equal weight-space geometry without a metric.

\subsection{What the server can and cannot change}

Constraint C3 (one global model, no personalisation) forecloses the structural
repair --- private per-client subspaces --- and so removes personalised
federated learning from the design space. It does \emph{not} leave the
aggregation direction as the only lever. Two remain: the \textbf{aggregation
rule} (weights, direction, step norm) and the \textbf{local-work budget} (steps
per client per round). Both are shown below to move the outcome, and the model
must account for both.

We parameterise the weighting by an exponent,
\begin{equation}
v_k \;\propto\; n_k^{\,q},\qquad q\in[0,1],
\label{eq:qweight}
\end{equation}
recovering uniform averaging at $q=0$ and size-proportional FedAvg at $q=1$.
Treating $q$ as continuous is what converts a two-arm contrast into a
dose--response test.

\subsection{Assumptions}

\begin{description}
\item[\textsc{a1} (first-order progress).] A client's per-round change in
retrieval quality is governed, to first order, by the component of the applied
global step along its own update direction:
$\Delta\mathrm{metric}_k \approx \phi_k(\langle \Delta W_{\mathrm{agg}},
u_k\rangle)$ with $u_k=\Delta W_k/\|\Delta W_k\|$ and $\phi_k$ increasing near
zero. This is the model's principal leap: client deltas are multi-step outputs,
not gradients, so \textsc{a1} is an approximation whose adequacy is an empirical
question, tested by \textsc{p1}.

\item[\textsc{a2} (received, not self, alignment).] What acts on client $k$ is
what the \emph{rest} of the federation sends. The governing quantity is the
leave-one-out alignment
\begin{equation}
a_k^{-k}\;=\;\cos\!\Big(\Delta W_k,\;\textstyle\sum_{j\neq k} v_j\,\Delta W_j\Big).
\label{eq:loo}
\end{equation}
The self-inclusive alignment $\cos(\Delta W_k,\Delta W_{\mathrm{agg}})$ is
\emph{not} a substitute: it is a closed-form function of $(v,\|\Delta W\|,C)$
that is pinned by $v_k$ alone. At $v_{\max}=0.875$ we measure it at $0.977$
under the true geometry, $0.978$ under an identity Gram, and $0.978$ under an
adversarial Gram in which the majority is anti-aligned with every other client
at $-0.3$. A statistic that cannot distinguish those three geometries carries
no information about geometry, and we do not report it as evidence.

\item[\textsc{a3} (local work drives divergence).] Increasing local steps per
round increases both the norm and the mutual divergence of client deltas,
lowering off-diagonal Gram cosines. Larger, less mutually agreeing deltas make
any fixed weighting more consequential.
\end{description}

\subsection{The mechanism}

Combining \textsc{a1}--\textsc{a2}: a weighting concentrates the aggregate on
whichever client carries the most mass, and every other client then receives a
step whose projection on its own direction is set by the off-diagonal geometry
rather than by its own contribution. As $q\to1$ the received alignment of
under-weighted clients falls toward zero and can pass below it, at which point
the global step moves those clients \emph{against} their own update direction
and they surrender ground already gained.

The mechanism makes no exception for the majority. Once the deltas are mutually
divergent, a client that dominates the aggregate is not thereby helped by the
others: its own leave-one-out alignment is what the rest of the federation
contributes to it, and that quantity is small or negative under exactly the
same conditions. This is why the model predicts the failure is
\emph{lose--lose} rather than a transfer from small silos to large ones.

\subsection{Predictions}\label{sec:predictions}

None of the following is a restatement of \S\ref{sec:phenomenon}. Each is
either a new configuration, or a quantity not used to build the model.

\begin{description}
\item[\textsc{p1} (dose--response).] Minority erosion $E_k$ is monotone
increasing in $q$ over $q\in\{0,\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac34,1\}$, and the
majority's own final score is \emph{non-monotone} in $q$, peaking strictly below
$q=1$. Two arms cannot establish monotonicity; the curve can. This is the
model's sharpest test, and a non-monotone minority curve falsifies \textsc{a1}.

\item[\textsc{p2} (local work $\to$ geometry).] Raising the local-step budget at
fixed $q$ lowers the minority--majority Gram cosine and lowers minority
leave-one-out alignment. The erosion ratio between work regimes motivated
\textsc{a3} and is excluded from scoring; the \emph{Gram} prediction is scored,
and is recoverable from saved checkpoints at no compute cost.

\item[\textsc{p3} (coordinate invariance) --- \textbf{TESTED: FALSIFIED}
(all three seeds; unanimous).] The registered prediction was that erosion
under $q{=}1$ replicates in the frozen-$A$ coordinate. It does not: at every
registered seed the same weighting yields positive drift (SciFact
$+0.051$/$+0.055$/$+0.048$, ArguAna $+0.011$/$+0.019$/$+0.010$ against the
backbone, versus $-0.070$/$-0.077$/$-0.076$ and $-0.025$/$-0.017$/$-0.015$
with both factors training). The erosion-persists rule fails 0 of 3. Per this item's own
pre-committed consequence, the mechanism is entangled with joint-factor
optimisation and the model as first stated was wrong. The amendment is in
\S\ref{sec:model-amend}; the falsification is reported as a result, not an
embarrassment --- it is the sharpest fact this section owns.

\item[\textsc{p4} (redundancy).] In a federation containing near-duplicate
silos, a rule reading only the Gram moves mass off the redundant block, while
any rule that is a symmetric function of per-client losses cannot --- it assigns
loss-tied clients equal weight by construction. Pre-registered before data
collection (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}) with a committed clone-block mass shift
from $0.75$ toward $\approx0.54$.

\item[\textsc{p5} (depth-graded damage).] Erosion is graded in depth: shallow
metrics lose several times more than deep recall, because a relevant document
leaves $\mathrm{recall}@K$ only by crossing the depth-$K$ boundary while
shallow order flips on small adjacent margins (conditional no-flip and
coverage lemmas, \S\ref{sec:theory}). Measured: ArguAna's recall@100
\emph{improves} while its nDCG@10 erodes at both seeds; SciFact's deep loss is
$4$--$7\times$ smaller than its shallow loss, not zero --- so the claim is a
gradient, not an exemption, and the end-to-end margin mechanism is measured at
query level rather than asserted.
\end{description}

\subsection{Amendment after the \textsc{p3} test}\label{sec:model-amend}

\textsc{a1}--\textsc{a2} survive with narrowed scope: within a fixed
parameterisation, received alignment governs \emph{who gains and who loses} ---
the ordering. In the frozen coordinate, $n_k$ remains the worst weighting for
the minorities (ArguAna $+0.012$ versus uniform $+0.057$ versus max--min
alignment $+0.110$ at seed 123), exactly as alignment predicts. What
\textsc{a1}--\textsc{a2} do not deliver is the \emph{absolute} harm: the
below-backbone destruction requires the joint-factor pathway that freezing $A$
removes. We therefore add:

\begin{description}
\item[\textsc{a4} (interaction).] The received-alignment mechanism composes
multiplicatively with parameterisation error: under exact aggregation
(shared fixed $A$), sustained mis-alignment costs a client relative gains;
under joint two-factor training, the same mis-alignment compounds through
inexact factor aggregation into absolute regression. Skew is necessary but
not sufficient for destruction --- skew acting through the factor pathway is
sufficient.
\end{description}

Two switches, either sufficient to remove the absolute component: exact
aggregation (freeze $A$), or de-skewed weighting ($q\to0$, uniform, or
geometry-aware). The remaining predictions (\textsc{p1}, \textsc{p2},
\textsc{p4}, \textsc{p5}) are unchanged: they concern the redistribution
mechanism, which the falsification left intact, and \textsc{p1}'s
dose--response now doubles as a test of \textsc{a4}'s trainable-coordinate
side.

\subsection{What the model does not claim}

It does not claim client deltas are gradients; \textsc{a1} is a first-order
proxy and \textsc{p1} is where it can fail --- as \textsc{p3} already did,
with the amendment that falsification bought recorded above. It does not claim the Gram
determines retrieval quality --- only that received alignment is the leading
term. It says nothing about optimisation across rounds: the aggregate direction
is re-solved every round and no cross-round regret statement is available. And
it does not predict that any particular repair improves mean retrieval quality;
\S\ref{sec:design} reports a design the model implies together with the metrics
on which it loses.
