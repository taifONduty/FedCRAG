\begin{abstract}
Organizations that cannot pool documents increasingly want to share retrieval competence: one dense retriever, adapted federatedly with parameter-efficient updates, serving retrieval-augmented generation over private, evolving corpora. In a search current to September 4, 2026, we found no prior study combining per-client and per-depth ranking analysis with federated, adapter-only, replay-free dense retrieval; this is a bounded ``to our knowledge'' claim, not a verified absence. Across the measured work-regime~$\times$~weighting grid, the capped uniform/$n_k$/corpus cells and the full-work $n_k$ and frozen-$A$ cells have three seeds, the full-work uniform and FedSpan cells two, and the historical max--min cells one. Uniform aggregation improves every client in the measured cells, whereas size-proportional FedAvg erodes under-weighted clients near the top of the ranking and is Pareto-dominated by uniform even for the largest client. With full local epochs, seed-42 size weighting drives the two smallest clients \emph{below the untrained backbone} (SciFact $-0.070$ and ArguAna $-0.025$ frozen-anchored) while the two largest still gain --- a transient round-1 spike fully surrendered by round 8. A pre-registered mechanism discriminator adjudicates the cause: repeating the same $n_k$ weighting in a shared frozen-$A$ coordinate --- which removes the factor-aggregation pathway by construction --- \emph{eliminates the absolute harm} at every registered seed (SciFact $+0.051$/$+0.055$/$+0.048$, ArguAna $+0.011$/$+0.019$/$+0.010$ against the backbone, all three registered seeds clean, so the pre-registered withdrawal executed unanimously), while within that coordinate $n_k$ remains the worst weighting for the minorities. The destruction is therefore an \emph{interaction}: weighting skew alone redistributes; weighting skew compounded with joint two-factor training destroys, and either exact aggregation (freezing $A$) or de-skewed weighting removes the absolute component. A pre-registered query-level replay of the saved states shows how the damage lands: relevant documents leave the top ten from thin-margin positions (four of four files), and $n_k$ raises that exit rate two- to three-fold at every margin level rather than targeting borderline queries; a second registered clause tying exits to the sign of leave-one-out alignment failed and is reported as such. A budget-matched local-only control sharpens the stakes and splits by size: under $n_k$ the three minority silos finish below what they would have reached training alone (two clean seeds each, corroborated by the historical seed), while the majority does not, because its own local training overfits past the second epoch; and uniform federation beats the equal-budget outside option only for that majority. Whether joining is rational depends on which silo you are. A per-round cosine-Gram max--min weighting intervention preserves the entire round-1 gain instead of surrendering it (a $+0.091$ frozen-anchored swing in that cell, robust to the statistic choice), while uniform remains the score co-champion. These historical runs trained both LoRA factors and applied unit-direction game weights to raw factor states. They therefore motivate, but do not yet validate, the submitted frozen-A, norm-consistent FedSpan method; corrected implementation, attribution reruns, external baselines, and multi-session evidence are required before the method claim is submission-ready.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has made the dense retriever shared infrastructure: the component that decides which evidence an agent, an assistant, or a pipeline ever sees. Many agent-memory systems absorb change non-parametrically through evolving stores, graphs, and consolidation loops, but some deployments also need encoder adaptation. Organizations such as hospitals, banks, and law firms may be unable to pool the underlying documents. Federated fine-tuning of parameter-efficient adapters is one possible middle ground: share LoRA deltas, not documents.

This paper asks what happens to each organization's ranking when those adapters are trained jointly. The closest literature is fragmented: named federated-RAG systems often federate inference or use static/end-point evaluation; named continual dense-retrieval systems such as L2R and QDC are centralized; and federated fairness/common-descent methods optimize differentiable client objectives rather than per-depth held-out ranking. Our dated search found no exact match for the combined setting. We therefore test the narrower question directly rather than claiming that every adjacent problem was previously unmeasured.

\textbf{In the measured grid, the weighting determines who pays.} Under uniform aggregation every client improves, including the seed-42 full-epoch cell. Under size-proportional weights, minority $\ndcg$ peaks and then erodes more strongly than deeper recall, and uniform Pareto-dominates it for every client including the one holding 87\% of the weight. With full local epochs, the seed-42 size-weighted cell destroys minority competence in absolute terms: SciFact and ArguAna finish \emph{below the untrained backbone} ($-0.070$ and $-0.025$ frozen-anchored) while the two large clients still gain, and SciFact surrenders a $0.150$ $\ndcg$ round-1 spike. The interaction is measured; its causal optimization explanation remains an experimentally testable hypothesis.

Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Phenomenon} (\S\ref{sec:phenomenon}): a per-client, per-depth characterization of federated retriever fine-tuning over a work-regime~$\times$~weighting grid. The capped uniform/$n_k$/corpus cells (historical code) and the full-work $n_k$ cell have three seeds; the full-work uniform cell has two; the historical max--min cells are seed-42-only. Findings are depth-graded minority erosion under skewed weights, Pareto dominance of uniform in this testbed, and a three-seed work~$\times$~skew interaction.
\item \textbf{Theory} (\S\ref{sec:theory}): an absolute margin-censored $\ndcg$ perturbation bound in score space, a strict two-threshold depth window, and exact fixed-factor aggregation algebra. These results do not imply signed held-out ranking improvement from client-update alignment.
\item \textbf{Mechanism} (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}): state-based evidence that size weighting captures the linear aggregate direction and that minority directional payoff becomes negative, alongside the full time-varying bilinear residual. These are descriptive mechanism diagnostics, not a causal loss-to-ranking theorem.
\item \textbf{Intervention and method candidate} (\S\ref{sec:repair}): historical ordinary-LoRA max--min weighting results plus a corrected frozen-A, orthonormal, norm-consistent FedSpan specification. The former are evidence for the intervention; the latter requires clean attribution reruns before it is an empirical contribution.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Continual and lifelong retrieval.} Named centralized systems include L2R~\cite{cai2023l2r}, FlowRAG~\cite{zhang2026flowrag}, CREAM~\cite{cream2026}, and QDC~\cite{goswami2025qdc}, which directly studies continual dense-retrieval drift and forgetting. They do not instantiate our federated C1--C3 setting, but QDC prevents a broad ``first ranking-level continual retrieval'' claim.
\paragraph{Federated RAG.} A mapping study catalogs federated-RAG designs and identifies heterogeneity and evaluation gaps~\cite{chakraborty2025fedrag}. FedE4RAG trains BGE retrievers with communicated model updates and specifies no adapter restriction~\cite{mao2025fede4rag}. Neither supplies our adapter-only, per-client/per-depth, replay-free multi-session setting; our multi-session validation remains pending.
\paragraph{Federated LoRA aggregation.} Fixed-factor FFA-LoRA~\cite{sun2024ffalora}, residual-corrected FedEx-LoRA~\cite{singhal2025fedexlora}, and stacked FLoRA~\cite{wang2024flora} are direct construction ancestors. Our historical rescue did not freeze a factor, so it cannot be relabeled as FFA-LoRA; corrected attribution is an open experiment.
\paragraph{Optimization, fairness, and ranking robustness.} MGDA and its multi-task/federated descendants~\cite{desideri2012mgda,sener2018mtl,hu2020fedmgda}, CAGrad~\cite{liu2021cagrad}, FairGrad~\cite{ban2024fairgrad}, FAMO~\cite{liu2023famo}, regularized maximin-correlation analysis~\cite{lee2016rmca}, AdaFed~\cite{hamidi2024adafed}, and CRAFT~\cite{wang2026craft} already cover minimum-norm, common-descent, or worst-improvement geometry. AFL adversarially optimizes a worst-case client-mixture risk~\cite{mohri2019afl}; q-FFL instead defines a $q$-fair transformed objective and its q-FedAvg solver~\cite{li2020qffl}. Goren et al., Wu et al., and Devic et al. study ranking robustness under document, word-substitution, or prediction perturbations~\cite{goren2018ranking,wu2022certified,devic2024stability}. Our claim is a retrieval-specific constrained composition and diagnosis, not invention of minimax weighting or ranking robustness.
\paragraph{Non-parametric memory.} External-store and graph-memory systems can absorb corpus evolution without cross-silo encoder training. They are adjacent deployment choices, not evidence that every deployment freezes its encoder. Our setting concerns organizations that decide encoder adaptation is necessary but cannot centralize data.

\section{Problem Setting}\label{sec:setting}
$K$ clients hold private, evolving corpora $C_k(t)$ and query--document supervision $P_k(t)$. They jointly maintain one retriever: a frozen backbone $f_{\theta_0}$ with LoRA adapters $\Delta W_\ell = \sigma_\ell B^{\rm raw}_\ell A_\ell$, where $\sigma_\ell=\alpha_\ell/r_\ell$ is the known PEFT scale. Constraints: \textbf{(C1)}~raw data never leaves silos --- only adapter parameters and $O(1)$ scalars are communicated; \textbf{(C2)}~no data replay across sessions --- only $o(|\mathrm{data}|)$ statistics persist; \textbf{(C3)}~one global model --- no personalization. Each round, every client trains its local adapter copy on its own pairs with contrastive InfoNCE and in-batch negatives. We explicitly compare capped local steps with full-local-epoch work; we do not imply one fixed-work convention for every arm. The historical runs aggregate both factors, while the candidate implementation shares and freezes $A$ and aggregates scaled $B$-deltas; its clean E0 attribution remains pending. The deployment-default weighting is $w_k \propto n_k$ (local pair count); we study $\{$uniform, $n_k$, corpus-size, and conflict-aware $w^\ast\}$.

\textbf{Instruments.} After every round we evaluate every client's ranking on its own held-out queries at multiple depths ($\ndcg$, $\rc{10}$, $\rc{100}$). For federation rounds the \emph{primary cross-arm statistic is frozen-anchored drift} (final minus the untrained backbone, which we verified to be arm- and seed-independent in every measured cell), with final scores reported alongside in every cross-arm table; per-client peak-minus-final erosion $E$; and depth structure. Round-1-anchored drift $\RoundDrift$ (final minus round 1) is reported only as a \emph{within-arm} trajectory statistic: round 1 is post-treatment, and we measured its anchor differing across arms by 4.7--96 seed-sd, which invalidates it for cross-arm comparison (worked example: the corpus arm's apparent deficit is 75.6--85.4\% anchor artifact, with two sign reversals against $n_k$ on recall metrics). Sequential and future session experiments reserve GEM-style BWT/Forget terminology for genuine task/session matrices.

\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}
We state the two results that organize the empirical sections; proofs, the aggregation algebra, and five further results appear in the appendix.

\textbf{Margin-censored perturbation bound.} For unit-normalized embeddings with per-checkpoint drift budgets $\varepsilon_q,\varepsilon_D$ and $\rho=\varepsilon_q+\varepsilon_D$: no document pair with score margin above $2\rho$ can invert, and per query
\[ |\Delta\mathrm{nDCG@}k| \;\le\; \frac{L_k}{\mathrm{IDCG}_k}\sum_{i<j}|g_i-g_j|\,\mathbf{1}\{m_{ij}\le 2\rho\}, \]
with $L_k$ the largest adjacent discount gap. Damage is \emph{censored by the margin profile}: only tightly separated pairs are at risk.

\textbf{Depth window.} Let $\alpha_k^{\mathrm{adj}}$ be the smallest adjacent score gap at ranks $r,r{+}1$ with $r\le k$ and unequal gains. Under the strict sufficient score-space window $\alpha_k^{\mathrm{adj}} < 2\rho < \beta_K$ (the coverage-boundary margin floor at depth $K$), there exist score perturbations within the budget that change $\mathrm{nDCG@}k$, while $\rc{K}$ cannot decrease. Equality at $\alpha_k^{\mathrm{adj}}=2\rho$ may create only a tie and depends on tie-breaking; embedding-space realizability needs an additional encoder-geometry argument. Empirical margin profiles, rather than this existence theorem alone, decide whether the depth-attenuation mechanism applies.

\textbf{Convergence of the frozen-span family.} Write $f_k(B) := L_k(W_0 + \sigma B A^\ast)$; if each $L_k$ is $L$-smooth then $f_k$ is $L_B$-smooth with $L_B = \sigma^2 c^2 L$ under $A^\ast A^{\ast\top} = c^2 I$ (transfer lemma, appendix). Consider the one-local-step, full-participation, exact-gradient instantiation: per round the server applies $B_{t+1} = B_t + \eta z_t$, where $z_t$ is the minimum-norm point of $\mathrm{conv}\{-\nabla f_1, \dots, -\nabla f_K\}$ and $\eta \in (0, 2/L_B)$ is constant. Then, with $c_\eta = \eta(1 - L_B\eta/2) > 0$: (i) \emph{every} client's loss decreases monotonically, $f_k(B_{t+1}) \le f_k(B_t) - c_\eta\lVert z_t\rVert^2$ for all $k$ simultaneously (by the minimum-norm variational inequality $\langle -\nabla f_k, z_t\rangle \ge \lVert z_t\rVert^2$ and the descent lemma); (ii) if at least one $f_k$ is bounded below, $\sum_t \lVert z_t\rVert^2 \le D/c_\eta$ with $D = \min_k (f_k(B_0) - f_k^{\inf})$ over the bounded-below clients, hence $\min_{t<T}\lVert z_t\rVert^2 = O(1/T)$; (iii) every accumulation point $\bar B$ is Pareto-stationary, $0 \in \mathrm{conv}\{\nabla f_k(\bar B)\}$, since $B \mapsto \mathrm{dist}(0, \mathrm{conv}\{\nabla f_k(B)\})$ is continuous (the Hausdorff distance between the hulls is at most $\max_k \lVert\nabla f_k(B) - \nabla f_k(B')\rVert$) and equals $\lVert z_t\rVert \to 0$ along the iterates. \emph{Attribution:} this is a specialization of classical multiobjective steepest descent to the federated frozen-span coordinate --- the min-norm common-descent direction of Fliege \& Svaiter and D\'esid\'eri (whose projection inequality supplies the sharp factor)~\cite{fliege2000steepest,desideri2012mgda}, the constant-stepsize $O(1/T)$ criticality-rate template of Fliege, Vaz \& Vicente~\cite{fliege2019complexity} (see also Tanabe et al.~\cite{tanabe2023proximal}), whose federated one-local-step form coincides with the exact-gradient case of FMGDA~\cite{yang2023fmgda}; our additions are the frozen-span constant $L_B = \sigma^2c^2L$ and the explicit per-client constant $c_\eta$. Proof in the appendix, included for completeness.

Two boundary statements keep the theorem honest. First, it covers the \emph{ungated} iteration on all $K$ clients: the deployed activity gate (which excludes zero or non-finite updates) is a separate mechanism, and a gated-out client with a tiny nonzero gradient is not covered by (i) --- it can ascend by at most $\eta\lVert\nabla f_k\rVert\lVert z_t\rVert + (L_B/2)\eta^2\lVert z_t\rVert^2$ in that round, a quantity the gate threshold bounds. Second, the \emph{deployed} method applies the normalized direction $d^\star_t = z_t/\lVert z_t\rVert$ at the median client-update norm, which lies outside this theorem class \emph{by design}: the guarantee-bearing variant with step $\eta_t\gamma^\star_t$ instantiates FedMGDA+ at $\varepsilon{=}1$~\cite{hu2020fedmgda} (FedMDFG~\cite{pan2023fedmdfg} and AdaFed~\cite{hamidi2024adafed} develop the adjacent fair common-descent line) --- inheriting its Theorem 1b modulo assumptions multi-step LoRA deltas do not verifiably satisfy (globally bounded gradients; deterministic plain-GD local steps with vanishing local rate; vanishing weight deviation) --- at the price of a step that shrinks with the conflict level, the stall our step policy removes. We note defensively that FedMGDA+'s own theorems likewise analyze the unnormalized iteration, so the corollary inherits at exactly the strength their theory covers their deployed algorithm. On a two-client toy quadratic the $\eta_t\gamma^\star_t$ variant converges to the Pareto set while the constant-norm step locks into a bounded orbit around it; on the real grid, the measured $\gamma^\star_t \in [0.457, 0.644]$ with zero fallback rounds indicates the orbit regime, not the collapse regime, is what deployment trades for stall-freedom.

\textbf{Aggregation algebra.} Factor averaging deviates from matrix-space averaging by exactly the factor cross-covariance $\sum_k w_k (B_k-\bar B)(A_k-\bar A)$ --- zero when a factor is shared (sufficient, not necessary). With a frozen shared $A^\ast$ (orthonormal rows), aggregation is exact and linear in $B$; the Frobenius Gram matrix of communicated $B$-updates equals that of the true weight-space updates, which is what makes the server-side program of \S\ref{sec:repair} geometry-faithful while seeing only adapters.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
Four BEIR domains as clients --- NFCorpus, FiQA-2018, SciFact, ArguAna ($n_k \approx$ 110k/14k/0.9k/0.7k pairs; corpora 3.6k/57k/5k/8.7k docs) --- proxying medical/financial/scientific/argumentation silos. Backbone: Contriever (110M), selected by a headroom gate (independent fine-tuning must beat frozen on every domain; the gate rejects cells in which the chosen recipe lacks headroom --- our pilot's bge-m3 failed it on two of four domains). LoRA rank 16 on attention and FFN modules; MTEB-convention self-retrieval exclusion; per-round full re-encoding and evaluation. $n_k$ counts training query--document pairs, which for NFCorpus's graded relevance judgements (about 43 positives per query) yields a far larger skew than a query count would; we state both readings where the skew is used. ArguAna has no BEIR train split; its test queries are halved deterministically by sorted query id into train and evaluation halves, and every result file records this fallback. Under full local epochs the per-round step counts are 3455/442/28/21, so weighting skew and local-work skew coincide in this testbed; only the weighting is varied here. Seed coverage: three seeds for the capped uniform/$n_k$/corpus cells, the full-work $n_k$ cell and the frozen-$A$ discriminator; two for full-work uniform and FedSpan; one (seed 42) for the historical max--min cells. Provenance: the capped cells, the seed-42 saturation row and the one-epoch local-only controls come from a labelled dirty historical commit; the attribution grid, the replication seeds, the discriminator, the budget-matched controls and the replays run from pinned clean commits under an independent validator. The work-regime grid: \emph{capped} (500 local steps/round, $R{=}15$) and \emph{saturation} (one full local epoch per round, $R{=}8$). We report the pre-registered protocol (narrative branches and the saturation prediction were written before the data) in the appendix.

\section{The Phenomenon}\label{sec:phenomenon}
\subsection{Sequential baseline: mild, order-governed}
Sequentially fine-tuning one Contriever model through the four domains (three seeded orders) yields signed BWT of $-0.025$, $-0.003$, $+0.001$ $\ndcg$ --- real but mild in this measured cell, with damage concentrated when the largest domain trains last; peak-minus-final Forget is positive ($+0.048/+0.045/+0.023$). The separate bge-m3 pilot lacked headroom on two domains, so we make no cross-backbone claim about the severity of sequential forgetting.

\subsection{Federated: the weighting decides who pays}
Table~\ref{tab:capped} shows the capped regime. Under uniform weighting all clients finish above the untrained backbone (frozen-anchored drift $+0.113$ over three seeds; within-arm round-1-anchored drift $+0.072$--$+0.074$) and peak-to-final erosion is small but nonzero (maximum 0.00591). Under $n_k$, the under-weighted SciFact peaks at round 3 in \emph{all three seeds} and erodes ($E = +0.025/+0.033/+0.025$) while its $\rc{100}$ stays near-flat; under corpus-size weighting the under-weighted NFCorpus erodes in \emph{both} precision and recall ($E_{\rc{100}} = +0.011/+0.013/+0.011$ across seeds) --- on the one eroding client far from recall ceiling, recall falls nearly proportionally, which is why we name the structure \emph{depth-attenuated erosion} rather than a dissociation. Final scores place corpus much closer to uniform ($0.457$ vs.\ $0.468$ mean $\ndcg$) than the within-arm drift column suggests; corpus finishes ahead of $n_k$ on recall at all three depths on all three seeds. Erosion is forgone federation gain, not net harm versus going alone (eroded clients still beat their independent baselines; the single exception, FiQA under $n_k$, never reaches its independent baseline and we report it). Critically, in these cells uniform beats $n_k$ at round 15 for \emph{every} client, including the 87\%-weight NFCorpus.

\subsection{The interaction: work $\times$ skew}
Table~\ref{tab:grid} completes the grid. Saturation alone is positive under uniform weighting (frozen-anchored $+0.102$). Skew alone is damaging but bounded. Together, in the single-seed saturation cell, size weighting produces complete give-back: after a transient round-1 spike (within-arm $\RoundDrift=-0.053$), every client falls below its round-1 value by round 8, and the destruction is absolute for the minorities --- SciFact finishes $0.070$ and ArguAna $0.025$ \emph{below the untrained backbone} while the two large clients still gain ($+0.045$, $+0.064$; mean frozen-anchored drift $+0.003$). SciFact surrenders a $0.150$ $\ndcg$ round-1 peak. The interaction was a registered prediction, and the per-client absolute-harm reading now \emph{replicates on a second seed}: at seed 123 the same cell drives SciFact $0.077$ and ArguAna $0.017$ below the untrained backbone while NFCorpus and FiQA gain ($+0.045$, $+0.065$; mean frozen-anchored drift $+0.004$), and the third seed agrees (SciFact $0.076$ and ArguAna $0.015$ below the backbone at seed 2024).

The replication also supplies the control that makes the attribution causal. Holding seed, data, rounds and local work fixed and changing \emph{only} the weighting rule to uniform, every client finishes above the untrained backbone and the two minorities gain the most (SciFact $+0.098$, ArguAna $+0.100$; mean $+0.103$). Full-epoch training therefore does not harm small clients per se: the harm is attributable to size-proportional weighting. Uniform also dominates $n_k$ for \emph{every} client at this horizon, including the 87\%-weight NFCorpus ($+0.025$), replicating the lose--lose structure. Table~\ref{tab:e1} reports the cell.

\section{Mechanism}\label{sec:mechanism}
Two instruments dissect the failure: per-round update geometry, and a pre-registered intervention that removes one candidate cause by construction.

\textbf{Geometry: what weighting does to directions.} From per-round adapter states we compute the cosine Gram of effective LoRA updates. The honest per-client statistic is \emph{leave-one-out} alignment $\cos(\Delta W_k, \sum_{j\neq k} w_j \Delta W_j)$ --- the self-inclusive alignment of the dominant client is a closed-form function of the weights and norms (it reads $0.98$ under the true geometry, under an identity Gram, and under an adversarial Gram alike at $w_{\max}=0.875$) and therefore carries no information about geometry. Under $n_k$ at the saturation horizon, every client's leave-one-out alignment decays toward orthogonality or below (majority $-0.14$, SciFact $-0.05$ by round 15, seed 42): each participant is increasingly trained by an aggregate that the \emph{rest} of the federation points away from. This is why the failure is lose--lose rather than a transfer. The bilinear residual between the linear-mixture proxy and factor averaging is $0.071$ in weighted round 1, falling to $\sim 10^{-3}$ late; per-round alignment/next-round-$\ndcg$ correlations are mixed across clients and arms and are reported as descriptive only.

\textbf{Query-level margin replay (pre-registered).} The bound of \S\ref{sec:theory} says damage is censored by the margin profile; the registration committed a query-level test of it on the saved round states of the four trainable-coordinate saturation runs ($n_k$ and uniform, seeds 123 and 2024, $R{=}8$). Every saved state was re-encoded exactly as the driver evaluates, and the replay was gated on reproducing the recorded per-client $\ndcg$ (worst deviation $9.6\times10^{-4}$ over 36 states, frozen state exact), so it explains the reported scores rather than a re-run. Registered clause one --- relevant documents that leave the top ten come from queries in the lowest pre-round margin@10 quartile --- holds in all four files: exit rates fall monotonically from the thinnest to the widest quartile, with thin/wide ratios of $2.34$, $2.55$, $1.90$ and $2.80$, so the registered falsifier (a margin-independent ratio near one) did not fire. We note ourselves that the same gradient appears under uniform weighting: clause one establishes the proximate channel, not anything specific to $n_k$. Registered clause two --- more exits in rounds where the client's leave-one-out alignment is negative --- fails in all four files with the opposite sign ($n_k$: $0.049$ versus $0.101$; uniform: $0.019$ versus $0.025$, seed 123). It is confounded with time: exits are heaviest in round one and decay, while leave-one-out alignment starts positive and turns negative from round three; these runs cannot separate the two, and we drop the clause rather than rescue it. Two unregistered regularities hold at both seeds: $n_k$ produces four times uniform's first-round churn ($0.121$/$0.129$ versus $0.030$/$0.031$ of at-risk relevant documents exiting), and it multiplies the exit rate by $2.2$--$3.3$ at \emph{every} margin quartile, if anything more at wide margins. Size weighting therefore does not pick off borderline documents; it enlarges each round's perturbation of the score geometry, and margins decide which documents are the casualties within a round. The leave-one-out trajectories themselves are seed-stable to two decimals (majority $-0.158$/$-0.153$ at round 8 under $n_k$; under uniform three clients remain positive for six to eight rounds while SciFact turns negative from round four, reaching $-0.075$/$-0.072$). We note plainly what this does and does not support: under $n_k$ the two most anti-aligned clients are the two that gain, and SciFact gains most under uniform while anti-aligned, so leave-one-out alignment does not order outcomes \emph{across} clients; the ordering claim of \S\ref{sec:model-amend} is therefore not supported by these runs, and the interaction result below stands without it.

\textbf{Adjudication by intervention, not magnitude.} We had initially read the late $10^{-3}$ residual as evidence that factor-aggregation error is not causal. The pre-registered discriminator refuted that inference: rerunning the \emph{same} $n_k$ weighting with a shared frozen $A$ --- identical skew, factor-aggregation pathway removed by construction, canonical-validator-checked --- eliminates the absolute harm at all three registered seeds (SciFact $+0.051$/$+0.055$/$+0.048$, ArguAna $+0.011$/$+0.019$/$+0.010$ against the backbone, versus $-0.070$/$-0.077$/$-0.076$ and $-0.025$/$-0.017$/$-0.015$ with both factors training). A small per-round residual is not a small trajectory effect. Within the frozen coordinate the weighting ordering survives --- $n_k$ is still the worst arm for the minorities (ArguAna $+0.019$ under $n_k$ versus $+0.057$ uniform and $+0.110$ FedSpan at seed 123) --- so the corrected mechanism is an \emph{interaction}: weighting skew alone redistributes; weighting skew compounded with joint two-factor local training destroys. Either removing the factor pathway (exact aggregation on a frozen span) or removing the skew (uniform or geometry-aware weights) suffices to remove the absolute component. This places the factor-aggregation repair literature (FFA-LoRA, FedEx-LoRA, LoRA-FAIR) in a sharper light than our earlier reading: in this regime their fix is not incidental bookkeeping --- it is one of the two independent switches that turn catastrophic erosion off. All three registered seeds are complete and clean, so the pre-registered erosion-persists reading (both minorities below backbone at $\geq$2/3 seeds) fails unanimously: the registered withdrawal stands on the full sample.

\section{The Repair}\label{sec:repair}
The historical intervention solves $w^\ast=\arg\max_{w\in\Delta_K}\min_i(Gw)_i$ on a cosine Gram, then applies $w^\ast$ to raw trainable-A/B factor states. In seed 42, it reduces capped peak erosion to at most 0.0025, yields within-arm $\RoundDrift=+0.071$, and in the saturation cell preserves the entire round-1 gain rather than surrendering it --- a $+0.091$ frozen-anchored swing over $n_k$ ($+0.0909$ by final scores, robust to the statistic choice); uniform scores slightly higher in both cells. These are ordinary-LoRA intervention results from a dirty source snapshot. They do not instantiate the unit-direction vector certified by the LP. The new FedSpan candidate path instead uses a shared frozen $A$ with row-orthogonal, equal-norm rows ($AA^\top = c^2 I$, $c \approx 0.577$ matched to the initializer scale) and finite nonzero active deltas. Before geometry, each raw PEFT block is scaled by its known $\sigma_\ell=\alpha_\ell/r_\ell$ and the effective blocks are concatenated, so $r_k^2=\sum_\ell\|\sigma_\ell\Delta B^{\rm raw}_{k,\ell}\|_F^2$. With unit direction $u_k$, $G_{ij}=\langle u_i,u_j\rangle$, and declared true effective-update step norm $s$, it uses $c_k=s w_k^\ast/(r_k\sqrt{w^{\ast\top}Gw^\ast})$ and applies the same client coefficient blockwise: $B_{t+1,\ell}^{\rm raw}=B_{t,\ell}^{\rm raw}+\sum_kc_k\Delta B_{k,\ell}^{\rm raw}$ when the mixture norm is nonzero. These delta coefficients need not form a simplex and cannot be passed to direct client-state averaging. The candidate excludes nonfinite/zero/tiny clients, handles empty/singleton active sets, and fails closed with explicit logging on solver, cancellation, reconstruction, or declared coefficient-cap failures. Its finite-game payoff is an optimization diagnostic; it is not a signed nDCG guarantee. The current clone file is a cross-seed correlated-direction proxy, not same-broadcast clone-federation validation.

\subsection{Validation of the corrected method}
Two campaigns now establish the candidate. The first is an eleven-run attribution grid at seed 42 crossing the parameterisation (ordinary trainable $A{+}B$ versus shared frozen $A$) with the aggregation rule, plus a control isolating the row-scale of the frozen factor; every run passed an independent validator that recomputes each round's server aggregate from the persisted client states and refuses any disagreement. Within the frozen-$A$ coordinate --- the only comparison the grid licenses --- FedSpan finished ahead of uniform in both work regimes (final $\ndcg$ $0.451$ versus $0.435$ at full epochs; worst client $+0.064$ versus $+0.044$), with the historical raw max--min variant \emph{last}, so the gain is attributable to the corrected geometry and normalisation rather than to max--min as such. The same grid shows the row-scale control moving the score by $0.006$, about a third of the method's own margin, which is why cross-scale comparisons are excluded throughout.

The second campaign carries the method to the saturation horizon where the pathology lives, at two seeds (Table~\ref{tab:fedspan}). The mean advantage over frozen-$A$ uniform replicates closely --- $+0.0186$ and $+0.0166$ --- and its \emph{composition} is the informative part: the gain is concentrated on the two smallest clients (ArguAna $+0.053/+0.063$, SciFact $+0.018/+0.009$) while the two largest are approximately neutral, marginally positive at one seed and marginally negative at the other ($+0.002/-0.001$ and $+0.002/-0.004$). FedSpan is therefore a \emph{redistribution} rather than a uniform improvement: it does not promise majority gains, and maximising the worst client's alignment never implied it would. The mean is also close to the $+0.016$ measured independently at the shorter horizon. We note plainly that the aggregate advantage is driven by ArguAna; excluding it, the remaining three clients average approximately zero. Whether geometry yields a structural advantage that does not depend on a single client is precisely what the pre-registered clone federation tests.

The solver behaved as the theory requires, near-identically at both seeds. In all eight rounds of each run the solver's own convergence test reported the min-norm optimum reached (status \emph{optimal}, no fallback rounds); these runs predate the per-round Wolfe certificate, so the optimum is \emph{achieved, not certified}, and the protection level declined smoothly from $\gamma^\star = 0.644$ to $0.457$ (seed 123) and $0.641$ to $0.455$ (seed 2024) as clients differentiated --- throughout, more than five orders of magnitude above the fail-closed cancellation gate, so the direction-sensitivity regime discussed in \S\ref{sec:limitations} was never entered. Every client was still improving at the final round, in contrast to the $n_k$ trajectories that peak at round~1.

Two structural facts sharpen what the direction rule can claim. First, the protection level admits an exact characterization: $\gamma^\star = \mathrm{dist}(0, \mathrm{conv}\{u_k\})$, so $\gamma^\star > 0$ precisely when $0 \notin \mathrm{conv}\{u_k\}$ --- no linear-independence or definiteness assumption is needed, and a singular Gram is compatible with large protection. Second, the rule is \emph{clone-invariant by construction}: exact duplicates of a client direction leave the convex hull, hence the applied direction and $\gamma^\star$, unchanged, whereas uniform weighting over clients shifts its aggregate under duplication --- the asymmetry the clone-federation experiment tests in the near-duplicate regime.

We state the scope precisely. The within-coordinate margin is a two-seed measurement at this horizon; the shorter-horizon margin is a separate single-seed measurement; and the frozen coordinate itself remains behind ordinary LoRA on raw score at the shorter horizon (they are at parity at $R{=}8$), so freezing $A$ is justified by exactness of both aggregation \emph{and application} --- and, per the mechanism discriminator above, exactness is not cosmetic here: the frozen coordinate is the one in which $n_k$'s absolute harm does not occur --- an arbitrary aggregate step has no exact rank-$r$ factored representation when both factors train --- together with auditability and communication, rather than by accuracy.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
Single backbone family; $K{=}4$ proxy silos; the saturation-cell phenomenon holds on three seeds and its weighting-rule control on two, while the within-coordinate FedSpan margin is a two-seed measurement whose aggregate is driven by one client; dirty-code provenance applies to every capped cell, the seed-42 saturation row and the one-epoch local-only controls (the attribution grid, the replication seeds, the discriminator, the budget-matched controls and the replays run from pinned clean commits under an independent validator); no executed close common-descent baselines yet; no same-broadcast clone federation; and no completed changing-session evidence. The peak-then-erode phenotype is strongest on one client, recall ceilings limit depth analysis on two domains, and in the capped regime erosion is usually forgone gain rather than net harm --- the saturation regime is where it becomes absolute. The absolute-erosion phenomenon is coordinate-scoped: it requires joint two-factor training and does not occur under the same weighting in the frozen-$A$ coordinate (all three discriminator seeds clean). Of the two clauses of the pre-registered margin prediction, the leave-one-out-sign clause failed at all four replayed files and is reported as a miss, not reinterpreted; the margin-quartile gradient it confirmed is also present under uniform weighting and is a channel, not a cause. And federation's value is budget- and size-sensitive: against an equal-budget local-only control, the majority's alternative is overfitting alone (local-only peaks by epoch two, three of three seeds), so the worse-than-declining claim is refused there by our own comparator rule; for the minorities, uniform federation wins for some silos and loses for others (SciFact and FiQA train better alone at both completed seeds), so no claim is made that federation dominates non-participation --- only that $n_k$ weighting makes it strictly worse than non-participation for the three minority silos. No published fairness or common-descent baseline (q-FFL, AFL, FedNova, FedMGDA+) has been executed in this testbed; the FedMGDA+ native-step arm is built and pre-registered but unrun. We make no differential-privacy, secure-aggregation, Byzantine, partial-participation, cross-device, signed nDCG, or broad optimizer-novelty claim. Threat model, stated explicitly: C1 is a \emph{data-locality} constraint, not a privacy guarantee --- the server is trusted (honest), observes individual client adapters, and computes pairwise update geometry, which is structurally incompatible with sum-only secure aggregation; cryptographic protocols for computing the Gram without individual exposure are future work.

Three method-level limitations are stated with their evidence. \emph{Direction sensitivity:} the normalized direction rotates as $O(\varepsilon/\gamma^\star)$ under a perturbation $\varepsilon$ of one client direction, so small-$\gamma^\star$ rounds could apply a full-length step in a noise-dominated direction; in the measured runs $\gamma^\star_t$ remained in $[0.457, 0.644]$ --- more than five orders of magnitude above the fail-closed gate --- so this regime was not entered, and a soft-brake step policy is pre-registered as a contingent ablation should it appear. \emph{Stochastic weight bias:} the mixture is solved on directions produced by stochastic multi-step local training and is therefore a biased estimate of the population-level protective mixture~\cite{fernando2023moco}, on top of the delta-versus-gradient gap; a server-side correction in the MoCo style is future work. \emph{First-order license:} treating local update deltas as gradient proxies is the standard first-order license carried by the entire GEM/MGDA lineage~\cite{lopezpaz2017gem,sener2018mtl}; our alignment-to-next-round-score correlations are measured, not assumed, and the descent statement is confined to the exact-gradient corollary above.

\section{Conclusion}
The three-seed capped cells show a repeatable ranking-level warning: size-proportional aggregation forfeits gains that uniform federation provides, first near the top ranks. At the saturation horizon the warning becomes absolute and replicates on three seeds: minority clients finish below the untrained backbone under $n_k$ (SciFact $-0.074 \pm 0.004$, ArguAna $-0.019 \pm 0.005$) while a uniform control at the same seed lifts every client. The pre-registered discriminator attributes the harm to an \emph{interaction}: the same $n_k$ weighting in a frozen-$A$ coordinate eliminates the absolute erosion at all three registered seeds, so skew is necessary but not sufficient --- skew acting through joint two-factor training is what destroys. The corrected frozen-span method passed an eleven-run attribution grid under an independent validator and, at the saturation horizon on two seeds, redistributes toward the smallest clients (mean advantage $+0.018$, below the $0.022$ resolvable margin, concentrated on the two minorities, approximately neutral on the two majorities, and behind ordinary-LoRA uniform on worst-client score and cross-client variance), its solver's optimum achieved in every round. What remains before claiming a general cure is exactly the pre-registered remainder: faithful loss- and common-descent baselines, the same-broadcast clone federation where the geometry argument must earn its keep, and changing-session evidence.
